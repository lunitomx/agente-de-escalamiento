# S36.6 Design: Master Acceptance Ledger

## Design Summary

S36.6 creates one strict acceptance ledger for the remaining ESCALA Local V2
mission. It freezes the product-owner outcome into 42 observable requirements
owned by E37-E42 and gives later work one evidence contract. The initial ledger
is structurally valid but the future product is deliberately not ready: all 42
requirements begin as `unproved` until their named artifacts and gate receipts
exist.

The design separates two truths that must never be collapsed:

1. **ledger contract status** — whether the inventory and its authority bindings
   are complete, valid, deterministic, and safe; and
2. **mission readiness** — whether every requirement has fresh passing evidence.

A baseline may therefore report `contract_status: pass` and
`mission_readiness: unproved`. It may never report product completion from a
plan, source file, unchecked box, old roadmap label, or unexecuted command.

## PRIME and Complexity

The three required PRIME concept queries returned zero results. Code orientation
also returned no symbols for the branch. Four JIT keyword queries retrieved the
existing writer/validator contract patterns `PAT-L-14` and `PAT-L-6`; the other
matches were unrelated coaching/dashboard patterns and did not shape this
design. Zero or irrelevant results are recorded as such rather than converted
into invented precedent.

**Complexity: complex.** The implementation is small in runtime terms but
cross-cuts six future epics, three governance authorities, two evidence states,
human and machine renderings, and a downstream close-gate contract. False
completion and architecture drift are higher risks than algorithmic complexity.

- **Risk gate:** high consequence. A permissive state transition could falsely
  close the mission; a copied local-only vocabulary could drift from S36.5.
- **UX gate:** no new product UI or coaching interaction is implemented, so
  separate UX research is not required.
- **Integration gate:** this is an acceptance integration contract. A real
  repository baseline, committed-ledger source binding, two independent CLI
  runs, and rendered-file parity are mandatory; fixture-only tests are not
  sufficient.

### Duplicate and dependency sweep

- `validators/epic_closure.py` consumes closure dispositions; the new module
  will not replace or modify that consumer.
- Existing exposure, public-boundary, repository-truth, and public-export
  receipts prove their own bounded concerns. They are authorities or future
  evidence inputs, not substitutes for mission acceptance.
- No existing code executes verification commands stored in data. The ledger
  validator must keep this property: command declarations are data, never shell
  strings executed by S36.6.
- Direct importers of `validators/governance_contract.py` are limited to its
  tests, `validators/epic_closure.py`, and the governance CLI. S36.6 adds a
  consumer without changing their public behavior.

### Legacy sweep

Nothing becomes orphaned because no V1 acceptance ledger exists.
`work/epics/product-roadmap.md` intentionally coexists as a historical planning
artifact. The new ledger is the only acceptance authority for this mission,
while the roadmap remains non-authoritative and is not deleted or rewritten in
S36.6.

### Drift check

No `governance/drift-hotspots.json` or `governance/drift-catalog.md` is present,
so no ranked hotspot could be consulted. AG2 clone-amplification risk is
controlled by importing authority loaders/hashes rather than copying their
vocabularies. AG4 context-window risk is real because the story touches more
than three modules; it is reduced by one cohesive validator module, one thin
CLI, one policy file, one renderer, and one test module.

## Problem

The product owner has a concrete local-first business operating-system outcome,
but the remaining work has no executable requirement inventory. Existing code,
plans, old `DONE` labels, and dashboards cannot tell us which complete user
journeys actually work or what evidence would prove them.

## Value

After S36.6, any requirement can be traced from product intent to one epic/story,
observable behavior, expected artifact, verification command, gate set, platform
scope, and current proof state. This lets E37-E42 execute without losing scope
and gives the final audit a machine-verifiable definition of success.

## Solution Approach

Create one typed module that loads the ledger and the three existing authorities,
cross-validates their semantic hashes and invariants, evaluates requirement
evidence, and renders deterministic human/machine views. A thin CLI exposes
baseline and readiness modes. The committed Markdown is generated from the
typed ledger and must match the renderer byte-for-byte.

The validator never runs a command stored in YAML. A requirement declares a
safe argv vector and required gate IDs for review and receipt binding. Later
epics run those commands under their RaiSE lifecycle; a strict evidence receipt
records the command semantic hash and results. S36.6 only reads and verifies
receipts.

## Data Contract

All records use frozen strict Pydantic models and lowercase serialized enums.
Unknown, missing, null, unsafe, duplicate, or unsupported values fail closed.

```python
class MasterAcceptanceLedger(StrictModel):
    schema_version: Literal[1]
    mission_id: Literal["escala-local-v2-plan-maestro-2607202112"]
    authorities: AuthorityBindings
    source_requirements: list[SourceRequirement]
    epics: list[PlannedEpic]
    requirements: list[AcceptanceRequirement]
    limits: AcceptanceLimits


class AcceptanceRequirement(StrictModel):
    id: str                    # REQ-E37-001 .. REQ-E42-006
    owner: RequirementOwner   # matching E{N}/S{N}.{M}
    source_ids: list[str]
    acceptance: str
    evidence: EvidenceDeclaration
    platforms: list[Platform]
    architecture: ArchitectureClassification
    delivery_disposition: str # existing closure-disposition ID
    proof: UnprovedProof | ProvedProof


class UnprovedProof(StrictModel):
    state: Literal["unproved"]
    blockers: list[EvidenceBlocker]


class ProvedProof(StrictModel):
    state: Literal["proved"]
    receipt_path: str
    receipt_sha256: str
```

`proof` is a discriminated union. A proved record cannot contain blockers, and
an unproved record cannot carry a receipt. Workflow words such as `planned`,
`in_progress`, and `blocked` are not proof states; their canonical delivery
meaning belongs to the closure policy/backlog. Missing, stale, or failed proof
becomes a derived finding and never satisfies readiness.

### Required cross-field rules

1. Exact epic set is E37-E42 and exact requirement count is 42.
2. IDs are sorted, unique, contiguous within each epic, and match their owner.
3. Owner stories use the same epic number and appear in the planned-epic story
   inventory.
4. Every top-level source requirement is referenced by at least one requirement;
   unknown source IDs fail.
5. Acceptance text is non-empty, bounded, observable, and contains no absolute
   paths, URLs, shell syntax, or private sentinels.
6. Evidence and receipt paths are repository-relative, under the owning epic,
   and use an allowed suffix.
7. Verification commands are argv lists with fixed safe executables/tokens;
   there is no shell string and the validator never executes them.
8. Gate IDs are non-empty, sorted, unique, and include test/lint/format/type plus
   requirement-specific behavior where declared.
9. The loaded closure, identity, and public-export semantic hashes exactly match
   ledger bindings.
10. Public-export authority must still say local machine only, no hosted/cloud/
    OAuth/Drive/OneDrive API, no synced SQLite, legal review required, and
    publication unauthorized.
11. Every applicable requirement inherits installer-machine data authority and
    ordinary-filesystem exchange; contradictions fail.
12. A proved receipt must match requirement ID, evidence declaration hash,
    ledger semantic hash, source commit, platform set, gate IDs, artifact hash,
    and passing result. Missing, malformed, stale, or failed receipts block.

## Status and Exit Semantics

| Condition | Contract status | Mission readiness | CLI baseline | CLI readiness |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Valid ledger, 42 unproved | `pass` | `unproved` | 0 | 2 |
| Valid filtered epic, all proved | `pass` | `proved` | 0 | 0 |
| Valid ledger, any missing/stale/failed proof | `pass` | `unproved` | 0 | 2 |
| Invalid schema/authority/coverage | no receipt | no claim | 1 | 1 |

Exit 2 is a truthful readiness outcome, not an exception. Exit 1 is a sanitized
contract failure. No mode reports `pass` as a synonym for complete product.

## Determinism and Source Binding

The ledger is committed before live evidence is generated. The baseline receipt
then binds:

- ledger semantic SHA-256;
- closure, epic-identity, and public-export semantic SHA-256 values;
- the full verifier source commit containing the canonical ledger;
- exact epic and requirement counts;
- sorted proved/unproved IDs and findings;
- renderer SHA-256 for the committed Markdown view.

This two-commit flow avoids a self-referential receipt. Repeated baseline runs
from the same committed ledger must produce byte-identical JSON, Markdown, and
stdout. Absolute repository/temp paths, source text, matched values, company
data, URLs, and command output are forbidden from receipts.

## Lean Design Gates

- **KISS:** one ledger, one validator, one thin CLI, one renderer, one test
  module. No generic workflow engine, database, scheduler, or evidence service.
- **DRY:** import canonical authority loaders/hashes; do not repeat the closure,
  identity, local-only, license, or publication vocabularies.
- **YAGNI:** S36.6 reads proof but does not execute future commands, implement
  product behavior, initialize future epics, or automate external systems.
- **MVP:** exact inventory, strict truth model, deterministic render, safe CLI,
  and a real 0/42 baseline are sufficient to control E37-E42.
- **Value preservation:** domain value is the exact product requirement map and
  evidence qualification logic. A generic YAML pass-through would not prevent
  false completion, ownership drift, or hosted-architecture drift.

## Storage and Supply Chain Gates

No storage mechanism is replaced and no schema migration is added. Existing
SQLite/company-state behavior remains untouched. `rai schema sum check` passes
at schema version 32.

No external dependency is added. Pydantic and PyYAML are already declared and
used by the existing governance contracts, so supply-chain evaluation is N/A.

## Testing Strategy

| Layer | Scenario | Proof purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Contract | Canonical ledger loads with exact 6 epics/42 requirements and stable semantic hash | Complete inventory and determinism |
| Mutation matrix | Unknown/missing/null fields; wrong schema; duplicate/gapped IDs; owner mismatch; wrong counts; unused sources | Fail-closed schema and coverage |
| Authority | Closure/identity/public-export path or semantic hash drift; hosted/local-only/legal contradiction | No copied or stale authority |
| State | Unproved with blockers; proved without/malformed/stale/failed receipt; exact passing receipt | Plans cannot become proof |
| Security | Traversal, absolute paths, URLs, shell metacharacters, unsafe argv, private sentinel, oversized text/output | Safe data and receipts |
| Rendering | YAML-to-Markdown semantic parity and repeated byte identity | Human/machine agreement |
| CLI | Baseline 0, readiness 2, qualified readiness 0, invalid contract 1 | Truthful exit contract |
| Real repository | Commit ledger, run twice, compare bytes, confirm 0/42 and exact authority hashes | Non-mock integration |
| Regression | Governance contract, public export, repository truth, lint, format, types, schema v32 | No boundary regression |

Tests must exercise the writer output through the real loader/validator in one
flow, applying `PAT-L-14`/`PAT-L-6` rather than testing serialization in
isolation.

## Acceptance Criteria

### MUST

1. Canonical YAML contains exactly the six approved epic records and 42 sorted,
   unique, continuously numbered requirements above with matching story owners.
2. Strict typed validation rejects incomplete, contradictory, unsafe, stale,
   unknown, duplicate, or unsupported ledger/receipt data.
3. Existing closure, identity, and public-export authorities are loaded and
   semantic-hash bound; local-only/legal/publication contradictions fail.
4. Proof is discriminated: unproved requirements cannot carry receipts and
   proved requirements require exact fresh passing receipts.
5. Baseline and readiness modes expose distinct exit/status semantics; the real
   initial baseline is PASS/unproved with 0/42 and readiness exits 2.
6. Canonical Markdown equals the deterministic renderer byte-for-byte and both
   live baseline runs produce byte-identical safe receipts.
7. Focused/full RaiSE gates and architecture review pass before local merge.

### SHOULD

1. Epic filtering gives later stories a small deterministic readiness surface.
2. Findings use stable rule IDs suitable for later gate and PDF summaries.
3. The rendered ledger is readable in under ten minutes despite 42 entries.

### MUST NOT

1. Execute ledger-declared commands, add a hosted/cloud component, synchronize
   SQLite, call Drive/OneDrive APIs, or require OAuth.
2. Promote existing code, plans, roadmaps, retrospectives, or old `DONE` labels
   to proof without fresh exact receipts.
3. Claim legal approval, publication authorization, Windows qualification,
   mission readiness, or product-owner acceptance in S36.6.
4. Modify the public candidate, publish, push, rewrite history, or implement
   E37-E42 product capabilities.

## Decisions

### D1 — Exact 42-requirement mission baseline

The product-owner use case and E36 architecture are frozen into the inventory
above. Later epics may refine implementation but cannot silently delete or
weaken these outcomes; scope change requires an explicit ledger change with
review.

### D2 — Valid contract is not ready product

Contract validity and mission readiness are separate enums, fields, renderings,
and exit codes. This prevents a complete inventory of missing work from looking
like completed product.

### D3 — Declarative commands are never executed by the ledger validator

Verification argv and gate IDs are proof declarations. RaiSE lifecycle steps
execute them; the ledger reads independently produced receipts. This avoids an
arbitrary-command execution surface.

### D4 — Reuse authority semantics, do not copy policy vocabularies

The ledger imports closure and public-export loaders/hashes and binds legacy
identity policy. It adds only requirement-specific proof semantics.

### D5 — Two-commit live evidence flow

Commit typed contract and ledger/rendered view first. Generate and commit the
baseline receipt from that immutable source commit second. No self-referential
HEAD claim is permitted.

## Open Questions

None. File-format implementation details, OS versions, concrete future gate
implementations, and evidence production belong to the owning E37-E42 designs;
their observable outcomes and proof obligations are fixed here.
